## Top Themes This Month

1. **Price anxiety is universal.** Buyers across all price ranges express sticker shock and hesitate to commit without strong proof of value.
2. **Online reviews are not trusted.** Multiple buyers explicitly call out paid and sponsored reviews as a barrier to confident decision-making.
3. **Body-fit and size matching matters.** Tall, heavy, or broad-shouldered buyers actively search for chairs that accommodate their frame.
4. **Post-purchase support is a real fear.** Broken remotes, unresponsive manufacturer support, and difficulty finding repair technicians come up repeatedly in ownership discussions.
5. **SL-track and full-body coverage are key decision criteria.** Buyers want massage that reaches past the lower back into the hips and glutes, and treat this as a dealbreaker.
6. **Noise and vibration transmission is a practical concern.** Apartment dwellers worry about disturbing neighbors and ask what flooring solutions actually work.
7. **Brand financial stability raises red flags.** At least one buyer researched a brand's profit-and-loss statement before purchasing, worried about future service availability.
8. **Session length and interface usability frustrate new owners.** Short auto-off timers, intensity resets between sessions, and poor controls are common post-purchase complaints.

## Pain Point Breakdown

1. **Neck and upper back pain** (most discussed) -- Consistently tied to long daily sitting, desk work, and computer use. Buyers describe this as their primary reason for researching massage chairs, using language like "absolutely cooked" and "shoulders locked."

2. **Lower back pain** -- Mentioned across multiple discussions as a chronic, daily issue. Closely linked to the demand for SL-track coverage and hip-and-glute roller reach.

3. **Shoulder tension** -- Appears both independently and paired with neck and upper back complaints. Buyers specifically ask which chairs deliver targeted shoulder relief, not just general back massage.

4. **Gluteal tension** -- Cited as underserved by most chairs. At least one buyer identified full glute roller coverage as a dealbreaker, suggesting a meaningful unmet need in the mid-range segment.

5. **Chronic generalized pain** -- A smaller but notable group of buyers comes from a pain management mindset, comparing chair ownership to ongoing therapy costs and framing the purchase as a medical investment rather than a luxury item.
