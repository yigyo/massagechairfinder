Luraco, the Texas-based manufacturer behind some of the most clinically positioned massage chairs on the market, announced it has achieved Medicare approval, Durable Medical Equipment (DME) licensing, and FDA registration for its massage chair technology. The announcement was distributed via PR Newswire and marks what the company is calling a historic milestone for the medical massage industry.

## What Was Announced

According to Luraco, the company has secured three distinct regulatory recognitions: Medicare approval, classification as a licensed Durable Medical Equipment provider, and FDA registration. The company frames this as advancing "medical massage technology into mainstream medicine," positioning its chairs not merely as wellness products but as legitimate medical devices eligible for healthcare system integration.

Luraco did not specify in its announcement which chair models fall under these designations, what clinical conditions they are approved to address, or whether individual buyers can immediately begin filing Medicare reimbursement claims. Those details are pending further clarification from the company.

## What This Means for Buyers

This is the most significant regulatory development in the massage chair industry in years, and it matters to buyers in several concrete ways.

**Potential insurance and Medicare reimbursement.** If Luraco chairs qualify for Medicare coverage under the right clinical circumstances, some buyers, particularly seniors or those with documented medical conditions, may be able to offset the purchase cost through reimbursement. This would be a first for the category. However, buyers should not assume that any Luraco chair is automatically covered. Medicare DME coverage is typically condition-specific, prescription-dependent, and subject to coverage policies that vary by region. Buyers interested in exploring this path should speak with their physician and a Medicare benefits specialist before making a purchase decision.

**FDA registration raises the bar for accountability.** FDA registration is not the same as FDA approval of medical efficacy, but it does mean Luraco's manufacturing and products are subject to federal oversight and quality standards. For buyers who have long wondered whether massage chairs are held to any external standard, this is a meaningful answer. It puts Luraco in a different category than most competitors.

**Medical positioning vs. wellness positioning.** Most massage chairs in the [premium price range](/reviews/best-massage-chairs) are sold as wellness or relaxation products. Luraco has consistently marketed toward clinical use cases, including physical therapy support and chronic pain management. This regulatory development validates that positioning and may accelerate similar moves by other manufacturers.

**Who is the likely buyer now?** Luraco chairs have always appealed to buyers prioritizing durability, American manufacturing, and clinical credibility. The [Luraco i9 Max](/reviews/luraco-i9-max), for example, is frequently considered by buyers managing back pain, neuropathy, or post-surgical recovery. With Medicare approval in the picture, that buyer profile expands to include anyone working with a healthcare provider who might recommend or prescribe the chair as part of a treatment plan.

For buyers simply shopping for a high-quality massage chair, the regulatory news adds confidence in the brand's legitimacy without necessarily changing the purchase calculus. For buyers with documented medical needs and Medicare coverage, this announcement is worth investigating seriously.

## What We Do Not Know Yet

Several critical questions remain unanswered from the announcement:

- **Which specific models** are covered under the DME license and FDA registration is not stated.
- **Which medical conditions** qualify a buyer for potential Medicare reimbursement is not disclosed.
- **Whether a physician prescription is required**, and what that process looks like, has not been detailed.
- **Pricing implications** are unknown. DME certification sometimes changes how products are priced or distributed.
- **Whether retail buyers can purchase directly** or whether the Medicare pathway requires going through a licensed DME supplier is unclear.
- The announcement does not clarify whether existing Luraco chair owners can retroactively seek reimbursement.

We have reached out for additional detail and will update this article as information becomes available.

## How It Compares

Luraco occupies a narrow space in the [premium massage chair market](/reviews/best-massage-chairs). Its closest competitors on clinical credibility and price positioning are brands like [Inada](/reviews/inada-dreamwave) and [Human Touch](/reviews/human-touch-super-novo), both of which have marketed wellness and therapeutic benefits but have not, to our knowledge, achieved equivalent Medicare or FDA recognition.

Buyers comparing Luraco to brands like [Osaki](/reviews/osaki) or [Daiwa](/reviews/daiwa-supreme-hybrid) at similar price points will find that those chairs typically compete on feature density, track design, and massage program variety. Luraco competes on a different axis: clinical accountability, domestic manufacturing, and now formal regulatory standing.

For buyers who have been weighing a [Luraco i9 Max](/reviews/luraco-i9-max) against a comparable import-brand chair, this announcement may resolve the tie. The regulatory framework surrounding Luraco is now meaningfully stronger than any other consumer massage chair brand in the United States.

This milestone does not automatically make Luraco the right chair for every buyer. But it does make Luraco the most formally credentialed option in the category, which matters most to buyers with medical motivations for their purchase.
