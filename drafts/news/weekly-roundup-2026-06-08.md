The biggest development in the massage chair category this week has nothing to do with a new chair launch: [Luraco](/brands/luraco), the Arlington, Texas-based manufacturer, announced it has achieved Medicare approval, Durable Medical Equipment (DME) licensing, and FDA registration simultaneously. For buyers considering a therapeutic purchase, that combination of designations changes the conversation about what a massage chair can be in a clinical or insurance context. Elsewhere, the Infinity Circadian DualFlex picked up an editor's award, and several major consumer publications published their 2026 buying guides.

## Brand News

**Luraco earns Medicare approval, DME licensing, and FDA registration**

This is a meaningful triple milestone. Luraco Health & Beauty LLC, which the company describes as the only American manufacturer of medical massage chairs, announced on May 6, 2026, that it has secured Medicare approval, obtained DME (Durable Medical Equipment) licensure, and completed FDA registration. No other brand in the MCF review range holds this combination of designations, at least not publicly documented in the same announcement.

What does this mean for buyers? Medicare approval does not automatically mean your plan will cover a massage chair purchase, and buyers should consult their specific plan and physician before assuming reimbursement. What it does signal is that Luraco's chairs have cleared federal regulatory scrutiny at a level most consumer massage chairs have not pursued. For buyers with documented therapeutic needs, particularly those working with physical therapists or physiatrists, this credentialing gives Luraco a legitimate clinical conversation that competitors cannot currently match. The DME licensing is especially notable because it creates a pathway for Luraco chairs to be prescribed and potentially reimbursed as medical equipment rather than purchased as consumer goods.

If you are already researching Luraco's lineup for pain management or post-surgical recovery, this development is worth discussing with your healthcare provider before your next appointment.

**Infinity Circadian DualFlex wins International Business Times Editor's Pick**

[Infinity](/brands/infinity) announced that its Circadian DualFlex received the International Business Times 2026 Editor's Pick for Best Massage Chair. Award recognition from a mainstream business publication carries modest but real weight, primarily because it signals that a chair performs well enough to hold up under editorial scrutiny outside the massage chair enthusiast space. MCF does not yet have a full verified spec review of the Circadian DualFlex, but we are tracking the model and will publish our own assessment once we can confirm its [track type](/learn/track-types), [roller configuration](/learn/roller-dimensions), and pricing independently. If the Circadian DualFlex is on your shortlist, treat the award as a positive signal while waiting for MCF's hands-on notes.

**Bodyfriend expands wellness platform integration**

[Bodyfriend](/brands/bodyfriend), the South Korean brand known for its design-forward approach to massage chairs, was covered in trade press this week in connection with a broader industry trend: integrating massage chairs with adjacent healthcare and wellness devices into unified platforms. The reporting, via Aju Press, is more thematic than product-specific, and MCF does not have verified details on specific new products or pricing from Bodyfriend at this time. The trend itself is real. Several major brands are moving toward chairs that sync with biometric monitors or app-based health tracking, and Bodyfriend has historically been among the more aggressive brands in pursuing that direction. We will cover specific product details when they are confirmed.
