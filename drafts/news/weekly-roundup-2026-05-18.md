The biggest story this week has nothing to do with a new chair. [Luraco](/brands/luraco), the only U.S.-based manufacturer in the premium massage chair space, announced it has simultaneously achieved Medicare approval, Durable Medical Equipment (DME) licensure, and FDA registration, a combination that no other brand in the Massage Chair Finder catalog holds. That news landed alongside two meaningful product launches: [Kyota](/brands/kyota) introduced the Konbi M728 DualPro 4D, and [Human Touch](/brands/human-touch) opened sales on the Super Novo 3.0. It was a busy week for buyers who are actively researching their next chair.

## New Chairs and Launches

**Kyota Konbi M728 DualPro 4D**

Kyota announced the Konbi M728 DualPro 4D this week, positioning it as a luxury-feature chair at value pricing. The "DualPro 4D" name suggests a [4D roller system](/learn/roller-dimensions) as the centerpiece of the massage experience, meaning roller speed and intensity can be modulated in real time rather than following a fixed program. Kyota has not released detailed specifications through verified channels beyond the press announcement, so we are treating specs as preliminary until the chair ships and we can confirm figures independently. Based on the brand's existing lineup, a price point somewhere in the $4,000 to $6,000 range would be consistent with Kyota's positioning, but no pricing has been officially confirmed. We will update our coverage once the chair is available for hands-on evaluation. Buyers comparing this to existing chairs in that range should keep an eye on [track type](/learn/track-types), which the current announcement does not clarify.

**Human Touch Super Novo 3.0**

Human Touch announced that the Super Novo 3.0 is now available for purchase, placing it within the brand's premium Ascent Series. This is a successor to the Super Novo line, which has been one of the more recognizable names in the upper tier of the category. The press release describes the chair as expanding the Ascent Series, but specific updates over the previous Super Novo generation have not been detailed in verified materials. Buyers researching the [Human Touch lineup](/brands/human-touch) should confirm current pricing and available colorways directly, as Ascent Series chairs have historically shipped in limited finishes. We will add the Super Novo 3.0 to the MCF catalog once we have verified specification data.

## Luraco Achieves Medicare Approval, DME Licensing, and FDA Registration

Luraco Health & Beauty announced this week that it has achieved all three designations simultaneously: Medicare approval, Durable Medical Equipment (DME) licensure, and FDA registration. This is the most significant regulatory milestone any massage chair brand has achieved in the U.S. market to date, and it has direct implications for a specific group of buyers.

**What Was Announced**

According to Luraco, the company has secured the following:

- Medicare approval, meaning Luraco chairs may now be eligible for Medicare reimbursement in qualifying clinical or patient contexts
- DME licensure, a designation issued to equipment suppliers and manufacturers that meet federal standards for medically necessary devices
- FDA registration, which indicates that Luraco's manufacturing facility and relevant products are registered with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration

Luraco describes this milestone as advancing "medical massage technology into mainstream medicine." The company has long marketed itself as the only massage chair manufacturer based in the United States, producing chairs in Arlington, Texas, and has positioned its products at the intersection of consumer wellness and clinical therapy.

**What This Means for Buyers**

For most consumers shopping for a home massage chair, Medicare approval and DME licensure are not factors they encounter. But for a specific and growing group of buyers, this announcement is genuinely significant.

Buyers with qualifying medical conditions may now have a pathway to partial or full reimbursement for a Luraco chair through Medicare, depending on how their physician documents the medical necessity and which chair model is involved. This is not a guarantee of coverage, and buyers should consult their healthcare provider and Medicare plan before assuming reimbursement applies. No other brand in the MCF catalog has previously achieved this combination of designations.

Clinical buyers, physical therapy practices, and rehabilitation facilities have long used massage chairs as adjunct therapy tools, but procurement through Medicare-reimbursable channels has been complicated. Luraco's DME licensing opens a more formal purchasing path for those institutional buyers.

General consumers benefit indirectly. FDA registration and DME licensure require manufacturers to meet documentation, quality control, and safety standards that go beyond what typical consumer electronics or wellness products must satisfy. A chair built to those standards represents a different tier of manufacturing accountability.

For buyers already considering Luraco for therapeutic reasons, such as recovery from surgery, chronic back pain, or musculoskeletal conditions, these credentials add a layer of third-party validation that press releases and star ratings cannot replicate. Luraco chairs are priced in the $5,000 to $9,000 range, which is consistent with the investment level that tends to attract buyers with genuine medical or long-term therapeutic needs. Explore the [Luraco lineup](/brands/luraco) or use the [Chair Finder](/finder) to compare Luraco models against the full catalog.

**What We Do Not Know Yet**

Several important details are not yet clear from the announcement:

- Which specific Luraco models are covered under the Medicare approval or DME designation. It is possible that only certain chairs qualify, rather than the entire product line.
- The reimbursement process and dollar amounts. Medicare coverage for durable medical equipment involves prior authorization, physician documentation, and specific billing codes. Luraco has not yet published consumer-facing guidance on how a buyer would initiate a claim.
- Whether this affects pricing or availability. DME-classified products sometimes carry different pricing structures than their consumer retail equivalents.
- Exactly which FDA registration category applies. FDA registration of a manufacturing facility is different from FDA clearance or approval of a specific device. The announcement does not specify which registration classification Luraco has received.

MCF will update this coverage as Luraco releases additional documentation.

**How It Compares**

No other brand in the [MCF catalog](/chairs) currently holds all three healthcare credentials. The [Luraco i9 Max](/chairs/luraco-i9-max) competes most directly with chairs like the [Infinity Circadian](/chairs/infinity-circadian) and upper-tier Osaki models in the $7,000 to $9,000 range. Those chairs offer competitive feature sets, including 4D rollers, body scanning, and zero-gravity recline, but they are manufactured overseas and carry no equivalent medical-grade credentials. For a buyer whose primary motivation is therapeutic benefit and who may be working with a physician or insurer, Luraco's certification stack now creates a distinction that feature lists alone cannot address.

For buyers whose interest is primarily in massage quality and value, the certifications do not change the comparative roller performance, airbag coverage, or body fit of any chair. Those buyers should still evaluate chairs on [track type](/learn/track-types), recline range, and fit for their body dimensions.

## Worth Reading

**Massage Magazine: Top 10 Chairs Ranked by 1,400+ Licensed Massage Therapists**

Massage Magazine surveyed more than 1,400 licensed massage therapists to rank the chairs most used in actual treatment rooms. The [full ranking](https://www.massagemag.com/top-massage-chairs-278650/) is worth reading with one filter in mind: 7 of the 10 chairs are professional portable massage chairs used for hands-on chair massage at events and mobile practices, a different category from the home-use reclining chairs MCF covers.

Of the full-body automated chairs on the list, two are in the MCF catalog. The [Infinity Genesis Max 4D](/chairs/infinity-genesis-max) ranked fifth with 4.63% of therapist votes, and the [Fujiiryoki Cyber Relax Pro](/chairs/fujiiryoki-cyber-relax-pro) ranked ninth with 3.23%. The Human Touch NovoFlex and WholeBody 7.1 also placed (ranks 7 and 8) but are not currently in the MCF catalog. For buyers who want to know how a chair holds up in a professional setting, the two MCF-listed models placing in the top 10 is a meaningful data point.
