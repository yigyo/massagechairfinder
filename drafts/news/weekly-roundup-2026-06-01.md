The biggest story in the massage chair category this week has nothing to do with rollers or tracks. [Luraco](/brands/luraco), the Texas-based manufacturer that has long marketed itself as the only American-made massage chair brand, announced it has simultaneously achieved Medicare approval, DME (Durable Medical Equipment) licensing, and FDA registration. For buyers who have wondered whether a massage chair could ever cross from lifestyle purchase into covered medical expense, this week's news is the most concrete answer the industry has produced.

## New Chairs and Launches

No new chair models were announced this week with sufficient specification detail to cover. The Nouhaus story below, under Brand News, touches on product activity at High Point Market, but no specific model names or verified specs have emerged from that event as of publication.

## Technology and Innovation

Nothing in this week's intake rises to the level of a standalone technology story with verified specifications. The Luraco and Nouhaus developments carry indirect technology implications, covered in Brand News below.

## Brand News

**Luraco achieves Medicare approval, DME licensing, and FDA registration.** This is a meaningful milestone for buyers who purchase a massage chair primarily for therapeutic reasons, whether managing chronic pain, recovering from surgery, or addressing a diagnosed condition. Massage Chair Finder (MCF) has followed [Luraco's](/brands/luraco) positioning as a medically oriented brand for years, but these three designations together represent a different level of institutional recognition. Medicare approval in particular opens a potential pathway for partial insurance reimbursement, something no other brand in the MCF review range currently holds for massage chairs. DME licensing means Luraco's chairs can be prescribed and distributed through medical channels. FDA registration means the FDA has reviewed the manufacturing facility and product classification, though it does not imply FDA clearance or approval of therapeutic claims. Buyers with qualifying diagnoses should ask their physician whether a Luraco chair could be submitted for reimbursement under their specific Medicare plan. We will follow up with more detail on exactly which models carry these designations once Luraco publishes that information.

**Nouhaus at High Point Market 2026.** [Nouhaus](/brands/nouhaus) presented what it described as restorative massage technology at High Point Market in late April, framing the product around the sedentary health crisis. High Point is primarily a furniture trade event, so the audience for this presentation was retailers and interior designers rather than end consumers. No specific model names or pricing have been confirmed in reporting we have seen. If new chairs emerge from this showing, we will cover them when specifications are available. For now, this signals that Nouhaus is actively developing product and positioning it within a wellness narrative rather than a pure relaxation one. Buyers researching the brand can start with the [Nouhaus brand page](/brands/nouhaus) for current lineup context.

**Ceragem on home healthcare decoupling.** [Ceragem](/brands/ceragem) received coverage this week after a marketing commentator praised the brand's strategy of "decoupling" healthcare delivery from clinical settings and bringing it into the home. The framing is conceptually interesting but thin on product specifics. Ceragem's existing lineup focuses on thermal massage beds rather than traditional massage chairs, so this is more a positioning story than a product story. We will continue monitoring for any new product tied to this direction.

## What We Are Watching

The Luraco Medicare and DME story will continue to develop, and the key open question is which specific models qualify under these designations, what documentation buyers need from their physicians, and whether any commercial insurance plans will follow Medicare's lead. That last question could take months or years to resolve, but it is worth tracking for buyers managing chronic conditions who are working within a healthcare budget rather than a discretionary one. On the product side, the silence from most major brands this week, including [Infinity](/brands/infinity), [Osaki](/brands/osaki), and [Human Touch](/brands/human-touch), likely reflects a quiet period before summer promotional cycles begin. The Nouhaus High Point showing suggests the fall lineup may include new models oriented around wellness recovery.
