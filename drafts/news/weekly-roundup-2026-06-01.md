The biggest story this week did not come from a product launch. It came from a regulatory filing. [Luraco](/brands/luraco) announced it has achieved Medicare approval, Durable Medical Equipment (DME) licensing, and FDA registration simultaneously, a combination that signals a meaningful shift in how at least one premium brand is positioning its chairs within the broader healthcare system. That story, plus an award win for Infinity, a certification milestone for Bodyfriend in Korea, and a useful wave of third-party reviews, rounds out a busy first week of June.

No new chair launches were confirmed by manufacturer announcement this week. [Nouhaus](/brands/nouhaus) did unveil what it is calling "restorative massage technology" at High Point Market 2026, framing the reveal around the sedentary health crisis and positioning the category as a corrective tool for desk-bound lifestyles. The company has not released model names, specifications, or pricing. Massage Chair Finder (MCF) will follow up once product details are confirmed. ## Technology and Innovation

**Bodyfriend Da Vinci AI earns Korean health ministry certification.** [Bodyfriend](/brands/bodyfriend) announced that its Da Vinci AI massage chair has received Digital Medical and Health Support Device certification from South Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety. In practical terms, this means the chair's AI-driven session logic has cleared a formal regulatory review in its home market, not merely a marketing claim of clinical benefit. Whether the Da Vinci AI reaches U.S. retail with the same certification framework is a different question. U.S. FDA registration and Korean MFDS approval operate under separate standards. MCF will confirm U.S. availability and any domestic regulatory status before adding the model to the catalog. For now, this is worth watching as a signal of where the higher end of the category is heading technically.

**Medical Breakthrough on zero-gravity efficacy.** [Medical Breakthrough](/brands/medical-breakthrough) published a blog post this week examining whether [zero-gravity positioning](/learn/zero-gravity) actually delivers the benefits brands claim. The short answer supported by the post is yes, with important caveats about recline angle and session duration. The mechanism: elevating the legs to roughly heart level reduces compressive load on the lumbar spine, which allows the roller track to work against relaxed musculature rather than tissue that is bracing against gravity. If you have been skeptical about zero-gravity as a marketing feature rather than a functional one, the post is worth five minutes.

## Brand News

**Luraco achieves Medicare approval, DME licensing, and FDA registration.** This is the most consequential brand news of the week. According to a PR Newswire release from the company, [Luraco](/brands/luraco) has cleared all three designations, making it the only massage chair brand MCF is aware of that holds this combination. Medicare approval means certain Luraco chairs may qualify for reimbursement under eligible coverage plans, though buyers should verify their specific plan details with a benefits coordinator rather than relying on the brand's general claim. DME licensing means the chairs are classified as durable medical equipment by licensed distributors. FDA registration means the manufacturing facility and device category have cleared federal review. No other brand in the MCF review range holds this combination of designations, which matters most to buyers purchasing for documented therapeutic or rehabilitative use. We will update relevant Luraco chair pages with this information once we confirm which specific models carry the designations.

**Infinity Circadian DualFlex named International Business Times 2026 Editor's Pick.** [Infinity](/brands/infinity) announced that the Circadian DualFlex received the International Business Times 2026 Editor's Pick award for Best Massage Chair. The award is trade press recognition rather than an independent consumer test, so MCF weighs it accordingly. That said, the Circadian DualFlex is a legitimate chair with a [dual-track system](/learn/track-types) that addresses a real fit problem for taller users. Pricing and U.S. availability should be confirmed directly before purchase. MCF will note this award on the Infinity brand page.

**Human Touch on chiropractic alignment.** [Human Touch](/brands/human-touch) published a post summarizing what chiropractors think about massage chairs as a complement to spinal adjustment therapy. The short version from the post: most chiropractors view regular massage chair use as a reasonable tool for maintaining the soft-tissue relaxation achieved during adjustments, particularly in the 24 to 48 hours following a session. The post is brand-produced content, not an independent clinical study, and MCF notes that distinction. Still, the underlying point about chairs working alongside, not instead of, professional care is accurate guidance for buyers managing back or neck conditions.

**Ceragem and the home healthcare narrative.** [Ceragem](/brands/ceragem) received favorable marketing commentary this week for what one analyst called a "decoupling" strategy, positioning its products as bringing clinical-grade wellness into the home rather than competing on traditional massage chair feature lists. The framing is interesting strategically but does not change what the products do. MCF will continue evaluating Ceragem chairs on the same performance criteria as every other brand in the category.
